\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}
To ensure the reproducibility, transparency, and comprehensiveness of this review, we adhered to a systematic literature review methodology tailored to the intersection of artificial intelligence and quantitative finance, designed in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA 2020) guidelines. The completed PRISMA 2020 checklist is provided as Supplementary Information. This section outlines our protocols for literature retrieval, inclusion/exclusion criteria, data extraction, and the dimensions utilized for synthesizing the gathered research.

\subsection{Literature Retrieval Strategy}
Our primary objective was to capture the state-of-the-art advancements in financial time-series generation, particularly focusing on the paradigm shift driven by generative AI over recent years. 

\textbf{Search Databases:} We conducted exhaustive searches across premier academic databases and preprint repositories to ensure broad coverage of both peer-reviewed and cutting-edge unpublished works. The primary databases included Google Scholar, DBLP, Web of Science, Scopus, and arXiv.

\textbf{Search Keywords:} To construct a robust search query, we combined terms related to the financial domain, time-series data, and generative tasks. The primary Boolean search strings included: 
(\textit{``financial time-series generation''} OR \textit{``synthetic financial data''} OR \textit{``market simulation generative model''}) AND (\textit{``financial diffusion model''} OR \textit{``financial GAN''} OR \textit{``time-series imputation finance''} OR \textit{``financial foundation models''}).

\textbf{Time Frame and Publication Types:} Given the rapid evolution of deep generative models, we restricted our primary search window to papers published or uploaded between \textbf{2021 and early 2026 (including early-access and preprints)}. We included articles from high-impact journals (e.g., \textit{Information Sciences}, \textit{Expert Systems with Applications}, \textit{Quantitative Finance}), top-tier computer science conferences (e.g., NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR, KDD, IJCAI), and highly cited arXiv preprints that represent significant methodological breakthroughs.

After full-text screening, the final corpus comprises 130 studies, each assigned to a unique primary task category: extrapolation (54), imputation (29), and synthesis (47). Multi-task or cross-cutting contributions are discussed in the relevant sections but are not double-counted in task-level comparison tables.

\subsection{Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria}
As illustrated in Figure \ref{fig:lit_dist}, to maintain a high signal-to-noise ratio and ensure strict relevance to the core theme of FTSG, all retrieved papers underwent a two-stage screening process (title/abstract screening followed by full-text review) based on the following criteria:

\textbf{Inclusion Criteria:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Papers that explicitly formulate and address the generation, imputation, or fully synthetic simulation of financial time-series data.
    \item Studies that propose novel generative architectures (e.g., GANs, diffusion models, LLMs) specifically adapted for financial contexts.
    \item Research that establishes unified evaluation protocols, benchmarks, or datasets for synthetic financial data.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Exclusion Criteria:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Purely predictive or forecasting studies that treat outputs strictly as deterministic point estimates without a generative, distributional, or probabilistic perspective.
    \item General time-series generation papers that lack an identifiable and empirically tested financial application (e.g., solely tested on weather or traffic data).
    \item Outdated surveys or methodologies that do not incorporate modern deep learning or generative AI techniques, unless necessary for historical context.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Data Extraction and Coding Protocol}
For each paper that met the inclusion criteria, we executed a structured data extraction protocol. Two independent reviewers (authors) coded the literature to extract key metadata, minimizing subjective bias. The extracted fields included:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Task Type:} Extrapolation, imputation, micro-level synthesis, or macro-level market simulation.
    \item \textbf{Model Family:} Statistical/probabilistic, discriminative deep learning, GAN-based, diffusion/score-based, or foundation/LLM-based models.
    \item \textbf{Data Modality:} Univariate vs. multivariate, frequency (high-frequency order book vs. daily/monthly returns), and conditioning variables (e.g., text, macroeconomic indicators).
    \item \textbf{Evaluation Metrics:} The specific quantitative and qualitative metrics used to validate the generated outputs (e.g., MSE, Wasserstein distance, stylized facts, downstream utility).
    \item \textbf{Code/Data Availability:} Whether the authors open-sourced their codebases or utilized public benchmark datasets.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Review Dimensions and Taxonomy Synthesis}
The extracted data were synthesized and are presented in the Results and Discussion sections along six unified analytical dimensions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Task Formulation:} Defining the mathematical boundaries and generative objectives of extrapolation, imputation, and synthesis (presented in the Problem Formulation and A Two-Level Taxonomy subsection).
    \item \textbf{Model Design:} Analyzing how specific generative architectures are engineered to overcome financial data challenges (detailed across the Extrapolation, Imputation, and Synthesis subsections).
    \item \textbf{Applications:} Examining how extrapolation, imputation, and synthesis support downstream quantitative workflows (presented in the Applications subsection).
    \item \textbf{Evaluation Protocol:} Critiquing how the financial realism and empirical validity of generated series are quantified (presented in the Evaluation Protocols subsection).
    \item \textbf{Datasets and Benchmarks:} Summarizing the empirical foundations upon which generative models are trained and benchmarked (presented in the Data Resources and Benchmarks subsection).
    \item \textbf{Open Challenges and Roadmap:} Identifying theoretical and practical bottlenecks requiring future research interventions (presented in the Discussion section).
\end{enumerate}

By rigorously adhering to this methodology, this survey transitions from a mere enumeration of literature to a systematic, evaluation-oriented resource paper, providing a highly reliable foundation for researchers in the field.
